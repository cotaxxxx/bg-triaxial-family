**GeoEnclosure: A Methodology of Interval Enclosure and Sign Determination Based on Problem Structure**

*Decide What to Measure. Enclose Values. Read Signs.*

Katsushi Furuta (DAYBREAK)

Research note v0.6, 21 November 2026 (in Japanese, with English title and abstract). Not peer reviewed.

## Abstract

To count stationary points, must one determine the sign of the first derivative one chose everywhere? This note calls GeoEnclosure the research methodology that works backwards from the intended conclusion. It chooses which quantity to enclose, and what that quantity's sign allows one to conclude.

The idea is illustrated with elementary examples:
- In a two-variable example, the difference of two partial derivatives excludes stationary points on a square without any subdivision.
- In a one-variable example, three correct enclosures show that the enclosure a computation needs depends on the claim to be proved.

The method is then examined on the study of stationary points of a cone-volume-weighted angle energy on volume-preserving spheroids. On the same 186 terminal boxes of the equatorial plane, two decision rules were compared:
- The sign of the normalised radial derivative H excludes roots in 56 boxes.
- Using also the radial curvature and a tangential fourth derivative, the remaining 130 boxes yield the crossing direction: a positive-radius root, if present, crosses downward. This is the information needed to bound the number of stationary circles.

The comparison concerns what can be concluded on a fixed partition, not computing time.

GeoEnclosure claims no new numerical technique. It combines established ones under four principles:
1. a representation fitted to the shape;
2. sign decisions by guaranteed enclosures;
3. independent regeneration and checking;
4. closing by analysis.

The verification conditions are those of the GeoEnclosure Standard v1.1. Every claim in the note is listed with its evidence, who checked it, and its state (checked / reproduced only / unchecked).
